# CPS+ — the X68000 MIDI soundtracks

Three packs carry the external-MIDI soundtracks of the Sharp X68000 ports,
as heard through a Roland SC-55:

| pack | MRAs | what you need |
|---|---|---|
| `ghouls_x68k_midi` | Ghouls'n Ghosts (X68000 MIDI Soundtrack), Daimakaimura (Japan) | your Daimakaimura X68000 disk images |
| `sf2ce_x68k_midi` | Street Fighter II' Champion Edition (X68000 MIDI Soundtrack), + Japan | your SF2' CE X68000 disk images |
| `ssf2_x68k_midi` | Super Street Fighter II (X68000 MIDI Soundtrack), + Japan, Asia | your Super Street Fighter II X68000 disk images |

plus, for all three, your own **Roland SC-55 ROM set** (original SC-55,
firmware v1.21).

Unlike the disc-based packs, there is no recording to copy off a disc: the
X68000 games ship MIDI sequences and the sound came from whatever module was
plugged in.  So the kit renders them, on your machine, exactly the way the
published packs were rendered — same disk data, same SC-55 firmware, same
emulator at the same revision, same conversion — and then checks the result
against the published pack hash like every other pack.

## What you supply

**Disk images.**  A `.zip` containing the game's floppy images in `.dim` or
`.xdf` form (the usual formats for X68000 dumps).  Only the disks holding the
sound driver and the MIDI bank are read; extra disks in the zip are ignored,
and a missing one is reported by name.  `.7z` archives also work if a
`7zz`/`7z` binary is on your PATH; a `.zip` needs nothing extra.

The MIDI bank is hashed before anything is rendered.  A rip whose bank
differs from the one the packs were built from stops there, by name, rather
than an hour later with a hash mismatch.

**SC-55 ROMs.**  A directory holding the five original-SC-55 v1.21 files:
`sc55_rom1.bin`, `sc55_rom2.bin`, `sc55_waverom1.bin`, `sc55_waverom2.bin`,
`sc55_waverom3.bin`.  They are checked against the known hashes before any
render starts.  The SC-55mkII set is a different instrument and is not
accepted.  Nothing in this kit downloads firmware.

Point `builder/discs.toml` at both (the `# --- X68000 ---` block), or pass
them with `--disc` like any other source.

## What gets built, and from where

The first X68000 pack you build fetches and compiles two open-source tools
into `builder/work/x68000/upstream/`, at pinned revisions so the render is
reproducible:

* **ValleyBell's ExtractorsDecoders and MidiConverters** — decode the games'
  S.P.S. sequence banks to Standard MIDI.
* **Nuked-SC55 (float build)** — a cycle-accurate SC-55 emulator, used
  headlessly to render each song to audio.  Built with its `rtmidi`
  dependency.

That needs `git`, `cmake` and a C/C++ compiler (Xcode command-line tools on
a Mac; `build-essential` + `cmake` on Debian/Ubuntu).  It is a one-time step
of a few minutes; later packs reuse the builds.  `ffmpeg` and `ffprobe`
(already required by the rest of the kit) do the conversion to FLAC.

Each tool keeps its own licence in its checkout.  None of their code is
bundled in this kit.

## Time and space

Rendering is real-time-ish emulation: about a minute of render for every
few minutes of music.  Roughly, on one core:

| game | songs | render time |
|---|---|---|
| Daimakaimura | 27 | ~10 minutes |
| SF2' CE | 46 | ~1 hour |
| Super SF2 | 63 | ~1 hour |

`make_packs.py --jobs N` renders N songs at once (default: half your cores),
which divides those numbers accordingly.  The raw captures are large
(32-bit float, 64 kHz) — allow a few GB free under `builder/work/` while a
game renders; they are deleted once the FLACs exist, and the FLACs
themselves (0.4–0.9 GB per game) go with the rest of `work/` when the build
finishes.

Interrupted?  Run again.  Finished songs are reused; only the missing ones
render.

## Reproducibility, honestly

The published packs were rendered on a Mac (Apple silicon) and rebuilt from
scratch there byte-for-byte.  The chain avoids the usual sources of drift:
the emulator is pinned to a commit and gets a private firmware directory
per song so no render can influence another; the sample-rate conversion
uses explicitly pinned settings; and the pack builders decode to PCM before
encoding, so the FLAC container itself never matters.

What has **not** been proven is that the emulator's floating-point output
is bit-identical on every CPU and compiler.  If it is not on yours, the
build tells you: the pack builders re-measure the render (frame counts,
the SC-55's silent-level offset, loop tags) and stop on any discrepancy,
and `make_packs.py` will not stage a pack whose hash differs.  You will
never end up with silently different audio on the card.  If you do hit a
mismatch on Linux or Windows, that is worth reporting — it is the one
result this kit cannot check for you in advance.

## By hand

`make_packs.py` runs all of this for you.  To drive it directly:

```
python3 builder/make_x68k_flac.py --game ssf2 \
    --archive "/disks/Super Street Fighter II (X68000).zip" \
    --sc55-rom-dir /roms/sc55 --jobs 4
python3 builder/build_pack.py ssf2-x68k-midi \
    --flac-dir builder/work/x68000/ssf2/flac/ssf2
```

`--game` is one of `daimakaimura`, `sf2ce`, `ssf2`; the matching
`build_pack.py` modes are `ghouls-x68k-midi`, `sf2ce-x68k-midi`,
`ssf2-x68k-midi`.  The individual stages live in `builder/tools/` and each
has `--help`.
